What Is an MSCI World ETF?

An ETF can give an investor exposure to a basket of securities designed to track an index.

📊

The Index

MSCI World is an equity index rather than a fund. MSCI says the index captures large and mid-cap representation across developed markets and covers approximately 85% of free-float-adjusted market capitalisation in each country.

📦

The ETF

An ETF is an investment product that can be structured to track an index. The ETF has its own fees, structure, trading arrangements and risks.

🌍

Broad Developed-Market Exposure

A fund tracking MSCI World can provide exposure to companies across multiple developed markets through one investment product.

⚖️

The ETF Is Not Identical to the Index

The return of an ETF can differ from the index because of fees, trading costs, taxes, currency effects, portfolio structure and tracking difference.

Accumulating vs Distributing MSCI World ETFs

One of the important differences UK investors may encounter when comparing ETFs is what happens to income generated by the underlying investments.

Accumulating

Income Reinvested

An accumulating ETF generally reinvests income received from the underlying investments rather than paying that income out as a cash distribution to the investor.

Distributing

Income Paid Out

A distributing ETF generally pays distributions to investors according to the fund's stated distribution arrangements.

Important

Compare the Fund Documents

The exact treatment of income, dealing arrangements, tax treatment and reporting should be checked against the ETF's official documentation before investing.

What Should You Compare When Choosing an MSCI World ETF?

There is no single feature that determines whether an ETF is appropriate. Comparing several characteristics can help you understand the differences between products.

1. Ongoing Charges

Check the fund's stated ongoing costs. Even apparently small differences in costs can matter over long investment periods.

2. Tracking Difference

Look at how closely the ETF has historically followed its benchmark. The ETF return does not have to exactly match the index return.

3. Fund Structure

Read the fund documentation to understand how the ETF obtains its index exposure and whether it uses physical securities, sampling or another structure.

4. Accumulating or Distributing

Decide whether you need distributions paid as cash or prefer an arrangement where income is reinvested within the fund.

5. Fund Size and Liquidity

Consider the size of the fund, trading activity and the spread between buying and selling prices. These can affect the practical cost of investing.

6. Currency Exposure

Do not confuse the currency in which an ETF trades with the currency exposure of its underlying investments. These are different concepts.

MSCI World ETF GBP and Currency Effects

A UK investor needs to understand the difference between the ETF's trading currency and the currency exposure of the underlying companies.

Trading Currency Is Not the Same as Currency Exposure

An ETF can trade in pounds on a UK exchange while holding shares in companies whose businesses and share prices are affected by currencies such as the US dollar, euro, yen and other currencies.

Therefore, buying an ETF in GBP does not automatically remove currency risk.

Some products may offer currency hedging. Hedging can change the way currency movements affect returns and can introduce additional considerations and costs.

Before investing, check the ETF's official documentation for its base currency, trading currencies, currency-hedging policy and underlying exposure.

MSCI World ETFs for UK Investors

The UK market provides access to a range of exchange-traded investment products. The important point is to research the specific product rather than assuming all MSCI World ETFs are identical.

🇬🇧 Check the Listing

Check which exchange the ETF is listed on, the currency in which it trades and whether your investment platform supports that market.

📄 Read the KID

Read the fund's key information and official documentation before investing. Check objectives, risks, costs, structure and income treatment.

💷 Understand GBP Costs

Consider dealing charges, platform fees and any currency conversion charges that could apply to your particular investment platform.

🏦 Check Provider Authorisation

Before using an investment provider, check the firm and the investment service carefully. UK investors can use the FCA's resources to research regulated firms and investment risks.

📈 Compare Tracking

Look beyond the name of the ETF and examine how closely its historical performance has followed the relevant MSCI World benchmark.

🔎 Research Before Buying

Consider your objectives, time horizon, ability to tolerate losses and the total cost of investing rather than choosing a product solely because it has performed well recently.

MSCI World ETF Risks

An ETF tracking MSCI World can provide diversification, but it is still an equity investment and its value can fall.

Market Risk

The underlying shares can fall significantly during market downturns. Investors can lose money and may receive less than they originally invested.

Currency Risk

Changes in exchange rates can affect the GBP value of investments holding assets exposed to foreign currencies.

Tracking Difference

The ETF may not produce exactly the same return as the benchmark index because of costs, taxes, trading and other factors.

Concentration

Although MSCI World contains many companies, market capitalisation weighting means the largest markets and companies can have a substantial influence on the index.

Liquidity and Trading Risk

The price you receive when buying or selling an ETF can be affected by market conditions, spreads and liquidity.

Future Returns Are Unknown

Historical performance cannot guarantee future results. Returns can be positive or negative and future market conditions are uncertain.

MSCI World ETF UK FAQs

Common questions about MSCI World ETFs and investing from the UK.

What is an MSCI World ETF?

It is an exchange-traded investment product designed to provide exposure to the MSCI World Index or a related benchmark. The exact structure depends on the individual ETF.

Is MSCI World an ETF?

No. MSCI World is an index. ETFs can be created to track the index.

What is the best MSCI World ETF in the UK?

There is no universally best ETF for every investor. Compare costs, tracking difference, fund structure, liquidity, income treatment, currency arrangements and the provider's documentation.

Are MSCI World ETFs available in GBP?

Some ETFs can be traded in GBP, but the trading currency should not be confused with the currencies of the underlying companies. Always check the specific ETF.

What is an accumulating MSCI World ETF?

An accumulating ETF generally reinvests income received from the underlying investments rather than distributing it as cash to investors.

What is a distributing MSCI World ETF?

A distributing ETF generally pays income distributions to investors according to its stated distribution policy.

Do MSCI World ETFs have fees?

Yes. Costs can include fund charges, platform fees, trading costs, spreads and potentially currency-conversion costs. Check the specific product and provider.

Can an MSCI World ETF lose money?

Yes. An ETF invested in global equities can fall in value. Past performance does not guarantee future returns.

Is an MSCI World ETF suitable for everyone?

No investment is suitable for everyone. Suitability depends on individual circumstances, objectives, financial position, time horizon and ability to tolerate losses.

Where can I research an MSCI World ETF?

Start with the ETF provider's official factsheet and key information document, then check the relevant investment platform and regulatory information before making a decision.

Which Countries Are in the MSCI World Index?

The MSCI World Index covers large and mid-cap companies across 23 developed-market countries. This guide explains which countries are included, how geographical diversification works and why the country composition matters to investors.

What Is the MSCI World Index?

MSCI World is a developed-market equity index rather than an investment fund.

23 Developed Markets

MSCI's current factsheet describes the MSCI World Index as covering large and mid-cap companies across 23 developed markets. The index is designed to represent approximately 85% of free-float-adjusted market capitalisation in each country.

Broad Global Exposure

The index provides exposure to developed equity markets across North America, Europe and the Pacific region. It therefore offers considerably broader geographical exposure than investing in the shares of a single country.

Not Every Country Is Included

MSCI World focuses on developed markets. Emerging markets such as China, India and Brazil are not part of the standard MSCI World Index.

Country Composition Can Change

MSCI periodically reviews market classifications and index composition. Countries can therefore be affected by changes to market classification, accessibility and index methodology.

How MSCI World Is Geographically Diversified

The 23 countries span several major developed-market regions.

🇺🇸 North America

The United States and Canada are included in the MSCI World Index. Because the US equity market is very large relative to many other developed markets, US companies can have a substantial influence on the overall index.

🇪🇺 Europe

European developed markets represented in MSCI World include countries such as the UK, France, Germany, Switzerland, Netherlands, Sweden, Italy and Spain.

🌏 Asia-Pacific

Japan, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore and Hong Kong are among the developed markets represented in the index.

Why Does Country Exposure Matter?

Knowing the countries represented in an index helps investors understand what they are actually gaining exposure to.

Market Concentration

Although MSCI World contains 23 countries, the countries are not necessarily represented equally. Market-capitalisation weighting means larger equity markets can have much greater influence on the index.

Economic Diversification

Companies across different countries operate under different economic conditions, currencies, regulations and interest-rate environments. Geographic diversification can therefore provide exposure to a range of economic conditions.

Currency Exposure

A UK investor should distinguish between the geographical exposure of the underlying companies and the currency of an investment product. Currency movements can affect sterling returns.

Developed Markets Only

MSCI World should not be confused with an index covering every country in the world. Emerging markets are excluded from the standard MSCI World Index.

What About UK Investors?

MSCI World includes the United Kingdom, but the index is much broader than the UK stock market.

MSCI World vs UK-only exposure

An investor buying a UK-focused investment is primarily exposed to companies listed or classified within the UK market. MSCI World instead provides exposure across a broad group of developed markets, including the UK, United States, Japan, Canada, Australia and major European markets.

However, an MSCI World fund or ETF can still have significant exposure to the United States because the index is market-capitalisation weighted.

UK investors should also consider the difference between the index return and the return of an individual fund or ETF. Charges, tracking difference, taxation, dividends and currency movements can affect the investor's actual result.

MSCI World Countries FAQ

Common questions about the countries included in MSCI World.

How many countries are in MSCI World?

MSCI describes the MSCI World Index as covering 23 developed markets.

Which countries are in MSCI World?

The 23 countries are Australia, Austria, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Hong Kong, Ireland, Israel, Italy, Japan, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Portugal, Singapore, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and the United States.

Is the UK included in MSCI World?

Yes. The United Kingdom is one of the developed markets included in the MSCI World Index.

Is the United States in MSCI World?

Yes. The United States is one of the 23 developed markets represented by MSCI World.

Is China in MSCI World?

No. China is not one of the 23 developed markets in the standard MSCI World Index. China is classified separately within MSCI's emerging-market index framework.

Is India in MSCI World?

No. India is not part of the standard MSCI World Index because MSCI World focuses on developed markets.

Does MSCI World include emerging markets?

No. MSCI World is a developed-market index. Investors seeking emerging-market exposure would need to consider an appropriate emerging-market index or investment product.

Can the countries in MSCI World change?

Yes. MSCI reviews market classifications and index composition. Changes can occur when markets are reclassified or when index methodology and investability criteria change.

Does MSCI World give equal exposure to every country?

No. MSCI World is market-capitalisation weighted rather than equally weighted by country. Larger equity markets can therefore represent a much greater proportion of the index.

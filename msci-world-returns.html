MSCI World Returns & Historical Performance

A detailed educational guide to MSCI World historical returns, annual performance, GBP returns, investment illustrations and the risks investors should understand when researching this global developed-market equity index.

MSCI World Returns: What You Need to Know

The MSCI World Index has experienced periods of strong growth, as well as substantial declines. Looking at historical returns can help investors understand how variable global equity markets can be over different periods.

MSCI World is an index rather than an investment fund. Its historical index return therefore should not automatically be treated as the return an individual investor would have received from a particular fund or ETF.

Important: Historical performance is not a guarantee of future results. The value of investments can fall as well as rise and an investor may receive less than they originally invested.

MSCI World Historical Performance

Why historical returns matter

Historical performance can provide context when researching an index. It can show how the market behaved during different economic conditions, periods of rising interest rates, market recoveries and major financial shocks.

Historical performance is not a forecast

One of the most important points when researching MSCI World returns is that previous performance cannot reliably predict future performance. Future returns could be higher, lower or negative.

GBP returns versus other currencies

A UK investor may see a different return from the headline return reported in another currency. Currency movements can affect the return of an investment measured in pounds.

Index return versus fund return

An ETF or fund attempting to track MSCI World can have fees, transaction costs, tax effects and tracking differences. Consequently, an investor's actual return may differ from the index's published return.

MSCI World Investment Risks

Market Risk

Global equity markets can experience substantial declines. MSCI World is not protected from market losses.

Currency Risk

Currency movements can affect the return experienced by a UK investor, particularly where an investment is not fully currency hedged.

Concentration

Although MSCI World covers many companies and countries, market-capitalisation weighting means the largest markets and companies can have a significant influence on performance.

Fees and Tracking Difference

Investment funds and ETFs can charge fees and may not perfectly match the performance of the underlying index.

MSCI World Returns: Key Questions

What has MSCI World returned historically?

MSCI World has produced both positive and negative annual returns. The exact result depends on the period, currency, return type and dates being examined.

Can MSCI World lose money?

Yes. An investment linked to MSCI World can fall significantly during a market downturn. Historical maximum drawdowns demonstrate that global equity markets can experience large declines.

Is MSCI World suitable for five years?

There is no universal answer. A five-year period can still include substantial equity-market losses. Suitability depends on an individual's objectives, circumstances, time horizon and risk tolerance.

Is MSCI World the same as the S&P 500?

No. MSCI World covers developed-market equities across multiple countries, whereas the S&P 500 represents large US companies. The two indexes therefore have different geographic exposure.

Does MSCI World include emerging markets?

No. MSCI World is designed to represent developed markets. Emerging markets are covered by separate MSCI benchmarks.

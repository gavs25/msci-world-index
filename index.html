What Is the MSCI World Index?

Understand what MSCI World actually represents before considering any investment product that tracks it.

🌎

Global Developed-Market Exposure

The MSCI World Index is designed to represent large and mid-cap companies across developed markets. It is a widely used benchmark for global developed-market equities.

📊

An Index — Not One Fund

MSCI World itself is an index. Investors cannot buy the index directly. Instead, funds and exchange-traded products can be designed to track its performance.

🏢

Thousands of Companies Over Time

The index provides exposure to a broad collection of large and mid-sized companies rather than relying on a single company or market.

⚖️

Market-Capitalisation Weighted

Larger companies have greater representation within the index. This means the index can become significantly influenced by the world's largest businesses.

Why Investors Look at MSCI World

The appeal of a developed-market global index is largely connected to diversification and long-term equity exposure.

🌎 Developed Markets

The index is designed around developed-market economies rather than emerging markets.

Approximately 85% of each country's free-float-adjusted market capitalisation is represented.

🏢 Large & Mid-Cap Companies

The index focuses on large and mid-cap companies within the markets it covers.

The exact number and composition of constituents changes over time as markets and index methodology change.

🇺🇸 Significant US Exposure

Because the United States represents a very large share of global developed-market equity capitalisation, the MSCI World Index has historically had substantial US exposure.

🔄 Index Rebalancing

Index constituents and weights can change over time. Investors should therefore understand that today's composition is not necessarily tomorrow's composition.

Potential Advantages

Reasons investors may consider products that track developed global equity indexes.

Diversification

Exposure is spread across many companies and developed markets rather than concentrating the entire investment in one company.

Long-Term Approach

Global equity indexes are commonly used by investors seeking long-term exposure to company growth and economic activity.

Simple Benchmark

MSCI World provides a widely recognised benchmark against which certain global equity investment products can be compared.

The Risks You Need to Understand

A professional investment guide must discuss the downside as well as the potential upside.

Market Risk

Global equity markets can fall sharply. The value of an investment tracking MSCI World can decline substantially.

Large Drawdowns

MSCI reports a historical maximum drawdown of approximately 57.82% for the index during the global financial crisis period.

Currency Risk

For a UK investor, the return in pounds can differ from the return of a USD-denominated index because exchange rates change.

Fund Costs

An actual ETF or fund tracking the index can have ongoing charges and tracking differences which reduce the return relative to the index.

No Guaranteed Return

Past performance does not guarantee future performance. Future five-year returns could be considerably higher or lower than historical averages — including negative.

Inflation

A nominal investment gain does not necessarily mean an increase in purchasing power after inflation.

MSCI World Frequently Asked Questions

Important questions to understand before researching an MSCI World investment.

What is the MSCI World Index?

The MSCI World Index is an equity index covering large and mid-cap companies across developed markets.

Is MSCI World a fund?

No. MSCI World is an index. Funds and exchange-traded products can be created to track the index.

Can I invest £100,000 in MSCI World?

An investor can potentially obtain exposure to the index through an eligible investment product, subject to the investor's circumstances, provider requirements and the product selected.

Could £100,000 become £200,000?

It is mathematically possible for an investment to double, but there is no guarantee that it will. The time required depends on the actual returns achieved.

What could £100,000 be worth in five years?

Nobody knows the future value. The website therefore uses hypothetical scenarios such as 5%, 8% and 10% annual growth to demonstrate compound mathematics rather than making a forecast.

Is this financial advice?

No. This website and the downloadable guide are intended for general educational and research purposes and are not personalised financial advice.

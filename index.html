What Is the MSCI World Index?

Understanding the index is the first step before considering any fund or ETF designed to track it.

🌍

Developed-Market Exposure

MSCI World is designed to represent large- and mid-cap companies across developed-market countries. MSCI says the index covers approximately 85% of free-float-adjusted market capitalisation in each country.

📊

An Index — Not a Fund

MSCI World is a benchmark index. Investors do not buy the index itself. Instead, investment funds and exchange-traded products can be designed to track its performance.

🏢

Large & Mid-Cap Companies

The index focuses on large and mid-cap companies in the developed markets it covers.

⚖️

Market-Capitalisation Weighted

Larger companies generally have greater representation, meaning the index can become heavily influenced by the largest companies in global developed markets.

Why Investors Consider MSCI World

The index can provide broad exposure to developed-market equities through a single benchmark.

🌍 Global Diversification

Exposure is spread across many companies and developed markets rather than relying on a single business.

🏢 Thousands of Businesses

The index represents a broad universe of large and mid-cap companies, although its exact composition changes over time.

📈 Long-Term Equity Exposure

Investors may use global equity indexes as part of a long-term investment strategy, depending on their objectives, risk tolerance and circumstances.

🇺🇸 Significant US Influence

Because US companies represent a large portion of global developed-market equity capitalisation, US companies can have a significant influence on MSCI World.

🔄 Changing Composition

Companies can enter and leave the index and their weights can change as market capitalisation and index rules change.

💷 GBP Considerations

A UK investor's actual return can differ from the return of the underlying index because of currency movements, fund structure and investment costs.

Risks You Need to Understand

A serious investment guide must explain both potential benefits and potential losses.

Market Risk

Global equities can fall sharply. An investment product tracking MSCI World can lose a substantial portion of its value during a major market decline.

Large Drawdowns

MSCI reports a historical maximum drawdown of approximately 57.82%. Investors must be prepared for significant temporary or potentially prolonged losses.

Currency Risk

The index and an individual investment product can have different currency exposures. GBP returns can therefore differ from returns measured in another currency.

Fees & Tracking Difference

A fund or ETF tracking MSCI World can have charges and tracking differences. The investor's actual return may therefore differ from the index return.

Inflation

A nominal investment gain does not necessarily represent an increase in purchasing power after inflation.

Future Returns Are Unknown

Historical performance cannot guarantee future results. Future returns could be considerably lower, higher or negative.

MSCI World Frequently Asked Questions

Important questions for anyone researching the index.

Is MSCI World a fund?

No. MSCI World is an index. Funds and ETFs can be designed to track the index.

How many countries does MSCI World cover?

MSCI describes the index as covering large and mid-cap companies across 23 developed-market countries.

Can £100,000 be invested in MSCI World?

Investors can potentially obtain exposure through an eligible fund or ETF that tracks the index. The suitability of an investment depends on the individual's circumstances.

What could £100,000 be worth in five years?

There is no reliable guaranteed answer. The calculator on this website demonstrates hypothetical compound-growth rates rather than predicting future performance.

Can MSCI World fall?

Yes. Global equities can experience significant declines. Historical drawdowns show that investors can experience large losses during severe market conditions.

Does this website provide financial advice?

No. This website and the paid report provide general educational information and do not constitute personalised financial advice.
